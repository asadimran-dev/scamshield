Check if it's fake.

Six quick checks for the stuff that fools people: AI photos, deepfake videos, phishing links, QR codes, dodgy PDFs and GitHub repos. No account, no cost — most checks run right on your device.

AI Image & Deepfake Check

Upload a photo. The AI looks for the usual giveaways: warped hands, plastic skin, bent text, seams around faces.

AI Video & Deepfake Check

Upload a video. A few frames are pulled out and checked for face-swap seams and features that shift between frames.

Phishing Link & Scam Message Check

Paste the link, or paste the whole WhatsApp / SMS / email message. It gets pulled apart and checked piece by piece.

QR Code Safety Check

Upload a photo or screenshot of a QR code. You see what's inside it first — nothing gets opened.

Malicious PDF Check

Upload a PDF. Its structure is searched for the things malicious PDFs hide: scripts, auto-run actions, embedded files, bad links.

GitHub Repo Safety Test

Paste a public GitHub repo link. Its files, install scripts, workflows and release downloads get checked for planted malware.

What gets checked

Media
Photos and video frames are checked by AI vision for generation artefacts and face-swap signs.
Links & QR
Addresses are checked against the tricks phishers use: look-alike names, hidden destinations, pressure wording. QR codes are read, never opened.
Files & repos
PDFs are scanned on your device. Repos are checked file by file — scripts, workflows and release downloads included.

Note: no detector is 100% certain. Treat results as strong hints, not proof — and when money is involved, verify through the official app or number yourself.
